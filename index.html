01 · The challenge

Credit systems reward the data people already have.

Traditional models depend on credit histories, collateral, formal income and financial statements. That works for the formally documented—and can systematically screen out micro-entrepreneurs, informal workers, sole traders and rural borrowers.

Financial exclusion is not always a lack of creditworthiness. Sometimes, it is a lack of conventional evidence.
01

Traditional models exclude

Thin-file borrowers often cannot produce the evidence conventional scoring requires.

02

Alternative evidence is fragmented

Psychometric, social and behavioral indicators are usually studied in isolation.

03

Institutions lack an adoption playbook

Predictive research rarely explains integration, training and risk governance.

04

Social capital is underused

Trust, networks and peer accountability lack standardized institutional measures.

05

Governance has not caught up

Bias, privacy, explainability and regulatory compliance need an integrated response.

The central proposition: alternative indicators do not operate in a vacuum. Their responsible use depends on the institution that interprets, governs and acts on them.

The theory beneath the model

Four lenses connect borrower behavior to institutional adoption.

01

Psychometric Trait Theory

Links traits such as conscientiousness, self-control and locus of control to financial responsibility and repayment intent.

02

Social Capital Theory

Explains how networks, trust, community norms and peer monitoring can reduce information asymmetry.

03

Prospect Theory

Shows how loss aversion, framing and uncertainty influence borrower decisions beyond rational-choice assumptions.

04

TOE Framework

Examines how technology, organizational capacity and the external environment affect adoption.

03 · Research design

One question needs more than one kind of evidence.

A pragmatic, mixed-method design moves from mapping the evidence, to testing relationships, to understanding the realities of implementation.

01

Systematic literature review

Map what is known—and where the evidence breaks down.

A PRISMA-guided review synthesizes psychometric, social-capital, behavioral and organizational-adoption research. Recent peer-reviewed evidence is prioritized, with foundational theory included where relevant.

02

Quantitative empirical study

Test whether alternative indicators improve lending decisions.

A vignette-based survey asks experienced professionals from banks, microfinance, fintech and cooperative lenders across South and Southeast Asia to evaluate standardized borrower profiles. PLS-SEM, correlation and hierarchical regression test three hypotheses.

Lending desk · session brief

You are the duty officer at an inclusive lending desk. Five thin-file and near-file applications land in your queue. Traditional credit data is thin or missing. Earlier choices carry forward—tokens, risk budget, and live desk meters shape what later files cost and allow.

  • Information tokens — you start with 6. Unlock a psychometric, community, or behavioral signal (base cost 1 token; carryover strain can raise costs).
  • Risk budget — you start with 100. Approvals consume budget; declines and governance holds do not.
  • Live meters — Portfolio risk, Inclusion, and Governance shift after each decision and can unlock constraints or relief on later cases.
  • Decisions — Approve, Decline, or Hold for governance (fairness / consent / explainability review). One rush file may carry soft time pressure.

Your choices under uncertainty are the instrument. Scores reflect how you play—not a diagnosis of ability or creditworthiness.

05 · Responsible by design

Inclusion without safeguards is not progress.

01

Consent

Clear information, voluntary participation and the right to withdraw.

02

Privacy

Anonymized identities, secure storage and removal of recordings after transcription.

03

Fairness, transparency, accountability and non-discrimination in lending.

Compliance with GDPR and applicable protections in participating regions.
