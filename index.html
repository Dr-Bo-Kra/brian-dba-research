01 · The challenge

Credit systems reward the data people already have.

Traditional models depend on credit histories, collateral, formal income and financial statements. That works for the formally documented—and can systematically screen out micro-entrepreneurs, informal workers, sole traders and rural borrowers.

Financial exclusion is not always a lack of creditworthiness. Sometimes, it is a lack of conventional evidence.

Addressing Key Research Gaps

  • Gap 1 — Limitations of Traditional Creditworthiness Models: They systematically exclude informal workers, rural entrepreneurs and micro-entrepreneurs without formal financial documentation.
  • Gap 2 — Fragmented Alternative Creditworthiness Research: Psychometric, social-capital and behavioral indicators are studied in isolation, with no integrated creditworthiness model for inclusive lending.
  • Gap 3 — Lack of Organizational Operationalization Frameworks: Predictive research rarely explains how institutions should integrate alternative indicators into approval processes, staff training and risk governance.
  • Gap 4 — Underdeveloped Social Capital Indicators: Trust, peer monitoring and community networks lack standardized measures for institutional credit appraisal and risk management.
  • Gap 5 — Governance, Fairness, and Explainability Challenges: Bias, privacy, transparency and regulatory compliance lack an integrated approach to responsible adoption.

Research Aim & Objectives

To examine how psychometric, social-capital and behavioural-economic indicators shape responsible inclusive lending decisions—and the governance, ethics and operational challenges of integrating them into institutional practice.

  • RO1: Systematically review and synthesize literature on psychometric, social-capital, behavioral-economic and organizational-adoption factors in alternative creditworthiness assessment.
  • RO2: Examine how psychometric indicators contribute to responsible inclusive lending decision-making.
  • RO3: Evaluate how social capital indicators relate to responsible inclusive lending decision-making.
  • RO4: Assess the predictive influence of behavioral economic indicators on responsible inclusive lending decision-making.
  • RO5: Explore lending organizations’ perceptions of adopting alternative creditworthiness indicators.
  • RO6: Identify the ethical, operational and governance challenges of integrating those indicators into inclusive lending decisions.

Keywords: alternative creditworthiness, psychometric indicators, social capital, behavioral economics, inclusive lending, organizational adoption, responsible lending, financial inclusion, PLS-SEM, governance.

The theory beneath the model

Four lenses connect borrower behavior to institutional adoption.

01

Psychometric Trait Theory

Links traits such as conscientiousness, self-control and locus of control to financial responsibility and repayment intent.

02

Social Capital Theory

Explains how networks, trust, community norms and peer monitoring can reduce information asymmetry.

03

Prospect Theory

Shows how loss aversion, framing and uncertainty influence borrower decisions beyond rational-choice assumptions.

04

TOE Framework

Examines how technology, organizational capacity and the external environment affect adoption.

03 · Research design

One question needs more than one kind of evidence.

A pragmatic, mixed-method design moves from mapping the evidence, to testing relationships, to understanding the realities of implementation.

01

Systematic literature review

Map what is known—and where the evidence breaks down.

A PRISMA-guided review synthesizes psychometric, social-capital, behavioral and organizational-adoption research. Recent peer-reviewed evidence is prioritized, with foundational theory included where relevant.

02

Quantitative empirical study

Test whether alternative indicators improve lending decisions.

A vignette-based survey asks experienced professionals from banks, microfinance, fintech and cooperative lenders across South and Southeast Asia to evaluate standardized borrower profiles. PLS-SEM, correlation and hierarchical regression test three hypotheses.

Quantitative Research Questions (Paper 2):

  • RQ2: How do psychometric indicators contribute to responsible inclusive lending decision-making? (Tests H1)
  • RQ3: What is the relationship between social capital indicators and responsible inclusive lending decision-making? (Tests H2)
  • RQ4: To what extent do behavioral economic indicators predict responsible inclusive lending decision-making? (Tests H3)
Sample
200–300 professionals
Research questions
RQ2–RQ4
Analysis

03

Qualitative exploratory study

Understand adoption, governance and ethical friction.

Semi-structured interviews explore institutional experience, operational integration, regulation, fairness and responsible AI. Themes are developed using Braun and Clarke’s six-phase approach.

Qualitative Research Questions (Paper 3):

  • RQ5: How do lending organizations view the adoption of alternative creditworthiness indicators in inclusive lending decisions? (Exploratory, no hypothesis)
  • RQ6: What ethical, operational, and governance issues do lending organizations encounter when integrating alternative creditworthiness indicators into inclusive lending decisions? (Exploratory, no hypothesis)
